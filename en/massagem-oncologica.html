What it is

Touch designed for every stage

Oncology Massage is massage adapted for people with cancer, during or after treatment. It isn't a single technique: it's a way of working. Pressure, positioning, duration and areas of the body are adjusted to the treatment, test results and how the person is feeling that day.

The goal is to ease discomfort, calm the body and mind, and give back the feeling of being touched with care, at a time when almost every touch comes from medical procedures.

Important: Oncology Massage is a complementary therapy. It goes hand in hand with medical treatment and never replaces it.

What touch can bring

Benefits patients often feel, and that studies with people in cancer treatment have been showing.

Less anxiety

The body steps out of alert mode. Slower breathing, a quieter mind, a pause in the middle of a routine of appointments and tests.

Better sleep

When the body is relaxed, it's easier to rest. And rest makes a difference in recovery.

Relief from discomfort

Helps ease tension, muscle aches and the heavy-body feeling that treatment can bring.

Less tiredness

May soften everyday fatigue, one of the most common and most difficult effects of treatment.

Reconnecting with your body

After surgery and changes in appearance, respectful touch helps you feel at home in your own body again.

Well-being and comfort

A moment that is yours alone, unhurried, where you are cared for as a person, not a diagnosis.

Every body responds differently. Results vary from person to person.

In Oncology Massage, touch goes beyond the physical. It is comfort, it is presence, it is respect for each person's story.

Safety first

Care in every detail

Every session is planned around where you are right now. Nothing is done on autopilot.

  • A conversation first. Diagnosis, stage of treatment, surgeries, medications and how you are feeling.
  • Clearance from your doctor. Sessions take place with the approval of the team overseeing your treatment.
  • Gentle, adjusted pressure. No force. The touch is light and changes according to how you respond.
  • Protected areas. The tumor site, recent scars, irradiated skin, catheters and ports are respected.
  • Care for the arm on the surgery side. If you have had lymph nodes removed, that side receives specific care.
  • A comfortable position. Pillows and supports so you feel at ease, lying down, on your side or seated.
  • Your body's pace. Sessions can be shorter on more tiring days. You can pause whenever you want.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

Can massage spread cancer?

This is a very common fear, but there is no scientific evidence that massage spreads cancer. What does exist are specific precautions, such as not massaging the tumor area and adapting the pressure, and that is exactly what Oncology Massage does.

Do I need medical clearance?

Yes. Before starting, we ask you to talk to your doctor. This ensures the session is safe for where you are right now.

Can I have it during chemotherapy or radiation therapy?

In most cases, yes, with adaptations. Some days call for extra care, such as right after a chemo session or when test results are out of range. That is why we talk before every session.

Does the massage hurt?

No. The touch is gentle, and you can ask to adjust or pause at any time. The idea is that you leave feeling lighter than when you arrived.
